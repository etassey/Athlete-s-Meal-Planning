🏃 Build the Athlete Plate

Quality • Quantity • Timing for an Endurance Athlete

👥 Your Group

📖 The Scenario

You are the nutrition planner for a college endurance runner (70 kg / 154 lb). The athlete will complete a 2-hour run at 7:00 AM.

Your job: build meals for the whole day with the right foods (quality), at the right time (timing), and enough food (quantity) to reach the daily carbohydrate and protein targets.
⏱️7:00 AM2-hour run
🍞420–700 gcarbs per day
🍗 84–119 g protein per day

🧭 How To Do This Activity

  1. Read the timing guide below. It tells you what kind of food belongs at each time.
  2. Pick foods from the Food Bank at the top. Colors show the nutrient type. Each food shows its serving size and grams of carbs (C) and protein (P).
  3. Drag each food into a meal box. On a phone or tablet, tap the food first, then tap the meal box. Need more? Add the same food again to count another serving. Tap a food in a meal to remove one serving.
  4. Read the feedback under each meal. A yellow ⚠️ tells you what is missing. A green ✅ means the meal fits its goal. Each meal also shows its carb and protein total.
  5. Watch your daily totals. Try to land inside the green zone for carbs and protein. Then click "Check My Whole Day" and copy your plates onto the whiteboard with pictures or drawings.

💡 Tips: Right before the run, choose foods that digest quickly (go easy on fat, protein, and fiber). A balanced plate has a carb, a protein, a fruit or vegetable, and a healthy fat.
